It started as a plan for three weeks exploring Scotland’s west coast and islands with my wife and our Labrador. Two weeks before setting off I fractured a bone in my ankle and tore three tendons, so climbing the mountains we’d planned wasn’t an option. We had to slow down and find other ways to keep ourselves entertained.

It turned out to be the best trip we’ve ever had. We found more than twenty beaches, each with its own character and Mellon Udrigle, for me, was one of the most surprising.

When we arrived the small car park was surrounded by fields of sheep with no view of the sea. We followed the very short track beside a narrow stream and, as we reached the beach, the stream opened out to the sea. So did the view, revealing golden sand and turquoise water with mountains on the horizon.

We’d planned to walk the headland but the wind was too strong and we turned back after twenty minutes. Instead we stayed on the beach, played with the dog on the sand, watched sandpipers feeding on the shore and wandered over the rocks to the east, where we could see An Teallach filling the horizon. Maybe I’ll get up there some day.
