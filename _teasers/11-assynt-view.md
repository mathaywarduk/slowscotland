Port a Bhaigh campsite faces west and, the night before, we'd spent a calm, clear evening watching the sun set over the sea. The next morning, we set off early to make the most of the day.

Partway through our three-week road trip, we'd decided it was time to attempt a mountain. My ankle had been recovering from a fracture and it felt like time to test it properly. We left as the sun was coming up and turned onto the narrow road heading away from the campsite.

We knew that stretch well. It's where you can see five of the Assynt peaks lined up on the horizon: Suilven, Canisp, Stac Pollaidh, Cùl Mòr and Cùl Beag. We'd climbed four of them before and Cùl Beag was the final one and our plan for the day.

What we didn't expect was how much the morning light would change everything. As the sun came up the landscape turned orange and golden brown, the hills layering behind each other in the haze, in slightly lighter shades. We stopped the van for photos, trying to take it in.

Just as we were setting off again a group of deer crossed the road in front of us. There must have been fifteen of them, walking slowly across and stopping on the hillside. We watched and took photos until they were out of sight.

We went on to climb Cùl Beag and complete all five, but the best part of the day had already happened on that quiet road. The climb itself might have been a mistake; I spent the next six months in physio, but it was worth it.
