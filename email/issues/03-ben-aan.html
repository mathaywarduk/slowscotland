Issue 3

Sunset from
Ben A’an

Ben A’an isn’t really a mountain, more a big hill, especially when compared to Ben Ledi or Ben Venue nearby. But its summit has one of the best views in the Trossachs, looking straight down Loch Katrine.

Just after the second lockdown we took a trip with friends, travelling through north-west Scotland, Skye and the Trossachs. We’d become a bit obsessed with sunrises and sunsets by that point, always chasing the best light, so we decided to go up Ben A’an late in the day. It’s a short climb, usually busy, but at this time we had the walk to ourselves. And within forty-five minutes we were at the top.

Just one other person was there, a photographer behind his tripod with his camera pointing down the loch. There were already signs of sunset in the sky, clouds in the distance were turning orange and the sun was dropping quickly.

Within fifteen minutes it kicked off. The clouds went from softly lit to deep red and the whole sky glowed like fire, reflected in the loch below. We stood there for a bit, in silence, and then made our way back down in the dark, wondering whether we’d ever see a better sunset.
 
Before you go
Getting there Ben A’an has its own car park. The path from here crosses the road and goes straight onto the hill.
Pack Bring a headtorch for the walk back. Once the sun goes down it gets dark quickly.
How long Allow at least an hour to reach the top.
Wrap up Take layers, it can get cold once the light has gone.
 
In the area
Stay
For camping, try Three Lochs Forest Drive. They have campervan and tent spots tucked in the woods and along the lochs.
Food
If I’m in the area, I always visit Mhor 84. Great food, relaxed atmosphere and the perfect stop after a long day outside.
