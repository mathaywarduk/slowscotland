Issue 1

Stac Pollaidh
at Sunrise

Stac Pollaidh has always stood out to me. It’s not the biggest hill in Assynt but the ridge looks dramatic from the road. It makes you want to see what’s up there.

To have the best chance of catching the sunrise, we set off in the dark. The four of us and our two dogs pacing quickly up the hill without stops. By the time we reached half way the sun was rising above Cùl Mòr and we stopped to take it in, with clouds rolling into the valley we’d just climbed out of.

At the summit was whiteout. But we could see the cloud shifting so we waited and our patience paid off. It only took a few minutes before it cleared, gradually at first and then suddenly opening out across Inverpolly with Suilven, Canisp and the hills beyond.

We went looking for the true summit but couldn’t find a safe way up. (Scrambling with dogs is not recommended!) What we found instead was an incredible view over Loch Lurgainn with small patches of inversion still sitting on the water below.

On the way down we spotted a couple of deer on the hillside. They kept moving ahead of us as we descended, keeping just enough distance between us to feel safe until eventually we lost sight of them.

Back in the car park before breakfast.
 
Before you go
Getting there A beautiful drive in the Assynt to the car park. Although if you’re going for sunrise, you’ll see nothing on the way there!
How long Allow 3 to 4 hours so you have time for photos.
When we went Sunrise. A tough start but worth it.
The ridge Finding a way to the true summit is difficult and you don’t need it for the views.
 
In the area
Food
Ullapool is 25 minutes away and has The Seafood Shack, try the battered haddock wrap after a long walk. There’s also Oak and Grain, a pizza restaurant that does takeaway and the best pizza I’ve had in the Highlands.
Photo opp
After the walk, drive towards Achnahaird and keep an eye on the rear-view mirror. The views back across to Stac Pollaidh and the rest of the Assynt are incredible.
