Issue 9

Returning
to Torridon

Torridon is one of the few places that keeps pulling me back. Every time I visit the weather, the light and the reason for being there are different, but the feeling isn’t. The loch, the mountains and the silence always feel the same.

The first time was on our honeymoon, driving the North Coast 500 in late September. We’d come over from Applecross, stopping constantly for photos, with Beinn Alligin and Liathach towering on the other side of the loch. As the sun set we pulled over and watched a heron on the shoreline, waiting for fish, until the last of the colour disappeared.

A few years later we found the same spot again on our west coast road trip. This time the sky was heavy with cloud, but my wife and the dog went swimming while I cooked by the campervan. Then, when they’d dried off, we sat outside and watched the moonlight on the waves, with everything around us in different shades of blue.

Another trip took us back to climb Beinn Dàmh. We’d had days of rain before, but woke up to sunshine, looked out of the van window and said “let’s climb that one.” The climb was pretty straightforward until the final section, a boulder field that, thankfully, looked worse from below than in reality. From the top the whole of Torridon surrounded us… huge mountains in every direction.

The last time, we climbed Maol Chean-dearg. We’d just sold our house in England and were spending a few months moving between cottages, using the time to walk and work. From the summit we could see Beinn Dàmh, Liathach and the rest of Torridon, lit up by short bursts of sun between the clouds, and a rainbow over the village.

Each visit felt different but they all shared the same quiet feeling. I think that’s why I keep going back.
 
Before you go
Take waterproofs The weather changes quickly in Torridon, so plan for everything
Taking it easy There are smaller, less strenuous walks around the loch that still offer incredible views
Be a good parker Parking is limited around Upper Loch Torridon, but there are safe pull-ins if you stop for photos. Don’t park in passing places.
 
In the area
Food
The Beinn Bar is great for a post-walk pint, and the Bo & Muc restaurant next door serves meals with views of the hills.
Stay
Torridon campsite is free and close to the public toilets. At the other end of the scale, The Torridon Hotel offers luxury stays but at a price.
