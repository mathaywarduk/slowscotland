Issue 12

Suilven from
Cam Loch

I get a bit obsessed with planning trips. Routes, photo spots and mountain climbs; I like to have a good idea of where I’m going, well before I get there.

When we were still living in England we’d planned a trip to Scotland with friends. We knew we wanted to climb Suilven and would probably take the main route from Glen Canisp, but when I was studying the map I noticed another path coming in from the south, starting from a tiny layby beside Cam Loch.

We decided to check it out the night before. We were staying nearby so waited until the evening and drove over as the light began to fade. We parked up, went through the gate onto boggy moorland and after only a few minutes we found it, the spot I’d marked on the map.

Suilven stood in front of us, the light catching its ridge while the loch reflected the sunset below. As the sun set behind the hills and turned everything gold, we followed the track down to a small beach, sat by the water and had a whisky while the light disappeared.

That night we realised both our dogs had sore paws so the hike up Suilven was off. We’d come back and do it another time. It didn’t matter, that view was enough for now.
 
Before you go
Getting there The layby south of Cam Loch is small, so arrive early or late in the day
Pack The ground around the loch can be very boggy; waterproof boots are a must
When to go The light is great in the evening when the sun is setting between Suilven and Cùl Mòr
 
In the area
Walk
There’s a trail along the northern edge of Cam Loch that gives changing views of Suilven as you walk round the loch. You could even attempt to climb it from here.
Photo opp
If you don’t mind a short climb, Cùl Mòr’s lower ridge gives a big view across to Suilven and it’s great in the golden hour.
